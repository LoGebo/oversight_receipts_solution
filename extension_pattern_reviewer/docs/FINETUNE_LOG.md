## 2. Evaluation protocol (pre-registered BEFORE training; do not change after seeing results)
1. Three arms on the same lines: (a) Qwen3.5-4B zero-shot (no tuning), (b) Qwen3.5-4B + LoRA, (c) Haiku 5.5 prompt v3. Keyword baseline as floor.
2. Data splits: synthetic train / synthetic validation (hyperparameters, early stop) / **never-trained** tests.
3. Never train on: the 32 receipts, the 144 held-out lines, anything from `data_eval/`. Check near-duplicates (exact and fuzzy) between synthetic train and those sets.
4. The held-out was used to choose prompts v1->v3, so it is no longer blind. Freeze a NEW untouched test set (100-200 lines, ideally second annotator) before training.
5. Metrics: exact category, macro-F1, restricted F1, end-to-end disposition through the same engine, recipient kind, JSON-valid rate, share of errors rated `low` (EXC-03), cost and latency per receipt.
6. Statistics: Clopper-Pearson/Wilson intervals, paired on the same lines, bootstrap; >=3 training seeds; non-inferiority margin fixed in advance (proposal: not more than 3 points below Haiku on the frozen set, restricted F1 not worse, JSON-valid >= 99.5 %).
7. Data curve: train with increasing sizes (e.g. 50 / 200 / 500 / 1000+) and plot.
8. Audit ~100 synthetic labels by hand and report the error rate. Synthetic test split is reported separately as "in-distribution, optimistic".
9. Also check the adapter did not degrade general ability (sanity prompts) and that confidence bands still work with EXC-03 (fine-tunes tend to be overconfident).

## 6. la empresa de referencia "the reference series" series: what it says and how we adapt it (all 3 parts read in full, 2026-10-28)
Source: la empresa de referencia Engineering, Oct 6-8 2026 (Parts 1-3). Pasted by the candidate. Facts below are the article's, not ours.
- Part 1 (build): LLM risk agent after the existing ML model + rules; bounded role (can block, cannot override an upstream block); output limited to a compact risk level for latency (P50 ~1.5 s with Opus, ~7,000-token prompt); online A/B: 30% fewer fraud transactions, 22% less fraud value.
- Part 2 (evaluate): fixed benchmark (16,140 transactions, 813 fraud), same task and decision policy for every candidate, deterministic scoring against verified outcomes; metrics precision, recall, F1 and dollar-weighted recall; newer Opus/Sonnet/GPT versions scored lower on recall/F1 than their predecessors. Lesson: choose models with domain benchmarks; evaluate under the existing decision setup first; check latency, reliability and cost.
- Part 3 (own): Qwen3.5-9B post-trained with RLVR (GRPO) using a deterministic outcome-based reward (plus format checks), reward balanced across classes, LoRA, SkyRL (Megatron backend + vLLM rollouts) on one RTX PRO 6000 96GB, under $100; compared base vs post-trained vs Opus 4.5 (F1 +9.6%, dollar-weighted recall +35.4%); served with Ray Serve LLM + vLLM, P50 0.683 s vs 1.515 s; lifecycle: version weights + prompt + decision policy together, monitor, tested rollback.

### What we keep and what we change (candidate decided stack: Apple Silicon, MLX; no PyTorch/GPU stack)
Keep (the article's discipline): domain benchmark with deterministic scoring; same engine/decision policy for every arm; three arms (base, tuned, frontier); a metric weighted by what matters; latency and cost per call measured; version model + prompt + engine together; rollback = switch provider.
Adapt: (1) our "dollar-weighted recall" = share of the dollars of correctly-routed violations (use amount_usd per line) alongside plain recall; (2) compact serving contract: the tuned model outputs only what the engine needs (category, confidence band, recipient), restricted is looked up; (3) the engine stays deterministic and the model stays a bounded classifier.
Differences to state honestly: la empresa de referencia trained on proprietary outcome labels with RL on an NVIDIA GPU; we have synthetic, by-construction labels and a 16 GB Apple-silicon Mac. Our reward IS verifiable (category equals the constructed label), so RLVR/GRPO is possible in principle, but it needs many sampled rollouts per prompt; whether it is practical in MLX on 16 GB is UNVERIFIED. Plan updated by FT-005: RLVR (GRPO) + LoRA is the main track; SFT is the fallback.
